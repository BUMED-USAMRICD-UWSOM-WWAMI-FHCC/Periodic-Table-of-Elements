Dielectric Reference Mapping for Human Somatic Architecture
-----------------------------------------------------------

In structural biophysics and biomedical electromagnetic modeling, biological tissues are treated as lossy dielectric materials. Because tissue-specific relative permittivity ($\varepsilon_r$, or the dielectric constant) is inherently frequency-dependent and dynamic, standardized database compilations assess these properties across specific diagnostic, therapeutic, and microwave frequency windows. [1, 2]

The reference matrix below outlines the complex relative permittivity values derived from the standard Gabriel dispersion relationships and compiled compiled in biophysical databases like the [IT'IS Foundation Tissue Database](https://itis.swiss/virtual-population/tissue-properties/database/dielectric-properties). Tissues are grouped systematically by metabolic and structural systems to assist in mapping systemic electrostatic kinetics and field profiling. [3, 4]

Structural Mechanics of the Permittivity Variance
-------------------------------------------------

The striking division between high-permittivity tissues ($\varepsilon_r > 40$, such as the pancreas, kidney, muscle, and blood) and low-permittivity tissues ($\varepsilon_r < 15$, such as cortical bone, fat, and bone marrow) is driven by the structural law of volume-weighted tissue hydration and ionic concentration. [5]

Because biological water molecules ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$) feature a dramatic permanent dipole moment, tissues with dense water matrices allow for extensive dipole reorientation under localized electromagnetic kinetic currents. Hydrophobic lipid structures (fat) or highly calcified boundaries (bone) lack these freely shifting dipoles, constricting the localized electrostatic field length and preventing high charge accumulation. [5, 6, 7]
