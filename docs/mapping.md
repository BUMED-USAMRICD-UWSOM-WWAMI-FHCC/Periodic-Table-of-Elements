## **Structural Mechanics of the Permittivity Variance**

The striking division between high-permittivity tissues ($\\varepsilon\_r \> 40$, such as the **pancreas, kidney, muscle, and blood**) and low-permittivity tissues ($\\varepsilon\_r \< 15$, such as **cortical bone, fat, and bone marrow**) is driven by the structural law of **volume-weighted tissue hydration and ionic concentration**.

Because biological water molecules ($\\text{H}\_2\\text{O}$) feature a dramatic permanent dipole moment, tissues with dense water matrices allow for extensive dipole reorientation under localized electromagnetic kinetic currents. Hydrophobic lipid structures (fat) or highly calcified boundaries (bone) lack these freely shifting dipoles, constricting the localized electrostatic field length and preventing high charge accumulation.
